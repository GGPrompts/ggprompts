Mega-Deal Monday

Amazon Pours Another Twenty-Five Billion Into Anthropic

Amazon commits up to $25 billion on top of its prior $8 billion stake — $5B now at a $380B valuation, $20B tied to commercial milestones — in exchange for Anthropic’s promise to spend $100B+ on AWS over ten years and secure up to five gigawatts of Trainium2/Trainium3 capacity.

THE AI DISPATCH · APRIL 20, 2026

Amazon announced it will invest up to $25 billion in additional capital in Anthropic, on top of the $8 billion already invested. $5 billion goes in now at a $380 billion valuation; the remaining $20 billion is tied to commercial milestones.

In exchange, Anthropic has committed to spend more than $100 billion on AWS infrastructure over the next ten years and secure up to five gigawatts of Trainium2 and Trainium3 capacity — positioning AWS as the primary training and inference backbone for the Claude model family.

The deal mirrors Amazon’s $50 billion OpenAI cloud deal from two months ago, completing a picture in which the two largest independent frontier labs now have hyperscaler capital partners — OpenAI with Microsoft and Amazon, and Anthropic with Amazon and Google.

At a $380 billion valuation, Anthropic trails only OpenAI among AI-native private companies. The deal tracks with Anthropic’s projected 2026 ARR of roughly $35 billion.

Analysts read the structure — capital plus commitment — as the new template for hyperscaler-AI lab relationships, replacing the simpler equity-plus-credits deals of 2023-2024.

Research

ICLR 2026 Outstanding Paper List Leaks Ahead of Rio Conference

The ICLR 2026 committee designated 10 Outstanding Papers for the Rio de Janeiro conference. Standouts: Common Corpus (largest ethical LLM pretraining dataset), Q-RAG (value-based RL training for multi-step retrieval embedders — superseding single-hop RAG), SafeDPO (constrained safety-balanced preference optimization without auxiliary networks), FIRE (stability-plasticity reinitialization), and MedAgentGym (agentic biomedical training environment).

The slate signals a field-wide pivot from single-hop retrieval to long-horizon agentic reasoning. Where last year’s Outstanding list rewarded clever prompting and single-turn benchmark wins, this year’s honorees are overwhelmingly about training systems that plan, retrieve, and act across many steps — with safety and data-ethics papers sitting alongside the capability work rather than trailing behind it.
